You want to know the number one
communication hobby that predicts
whether a relationship will thrive? It
is surprisingly simple but rarely
practiced consistently.
Welcome back friends. My name is Dr.
Yakov. I'm a licensed psychologist and
couple startups. Today we are talking
about a habit researchers say is one of
the clearest signs of a strong
longlasting relationship. And no, it is
not never fighting. It is not perfect
communication. It is something much more
simpler and more human. It is the
ability [clears throat]
to be curious instead of defensive.
Let's break it down what that actually
looks like. One, curiosity slows down
conflict. When you lead with curiosity,
you stop reacting and you start
listening. Instead of snapping back
with, why are you attacking me?
Curiosity shifts to tell me what is
really going on for you. That one shift
alone can transform an argument totally.
Number two, curiosity helps you
understand the feeling beneath the
reaction. Sometimes your partner isn't
upset about what happened, but what it
represents. Maybe they are upset about
running late, not because of the clock,
but because being late reminds them of a
childhood where no one showed up or they
felt let down. Curiosity lets you see
the whole iceberg, not just the tip of
it.
Third, curiosity builds emotional
safety. When your partner knows you're
not going to laugh at them or shame them
[clears throat] or call them dramatic,
they open up more. They soften. they
trust and it is really important to have
these qualities where they could feel
safe and secure and that that safety is
the foundation of emotional intimacy.
Four, curiosity interacts assumptions.
Let's be honest, most conflicts aren't
actually conflicts. They are
misunderstandings wrapped in emotion. A
simple, "Hey, is this what you meant?"
can prevent an entire unnecessary
misunderstanding or war. Fifth,
curiosity keeps the relationship
growing. No matter how long you've been
together, your partner is still
evolving. There are fears, their dreams,
triggers, needs, they shift over time.
Curiosity says, "I want to keep learning
about you." And that is what keeps a
relationship alive. So, if you want a
thriving relationship, choose curiosity
over defensiveness
every single time.
