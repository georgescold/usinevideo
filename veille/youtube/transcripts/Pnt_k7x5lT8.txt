# Why You Keep Repeating the Same Relationship Patterns
# The Productivity Illusion · 104 vues · 00:05:26
# https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pnt_k7x5lT8

Different person,
same pain.
If your relationships keep ending in
different ways, but somehow make you
feel the same things, abandoned,
anxious, unseen, too much, not enough,
that is not bad luck.
That is a pattern.
And most people don't notice the pattern
because they're looking at the wrong
thing.
They look at the person, the
circumstances, the way it ended.
But the real pattern is in the feeling.
The emotional texture,
the role you always end up playing,
the one who chases, the one who waits,
the one who over gives, the one who
tries to earn love, the one who feels
chosen at first and forgotten later.
That part usually repeats.
Not because you're broken,
because something in you is still trying
to solve an old emotional problem.
Psychology has a name for this,
repetition compulsion.
It means unresolved pain doesn't just
disappear. It recreates itself.
It finds new people, new situations, new
relationships, and quietly builds the
same emotional dynamic all over again.
Not to punish you,
to try one more time to finally get a
different ending.
That's why it can feel so confusing,
because each relationship looks
different on the surface,
but underneath,
it's asking the same question.
Am I enough for someone to stay?
Can I be loved without proving myself?
If I let someone really know me, will
they leave?
Will I always be the one who wants more?
That's the part people miss.
You're not just choosing a person,
you're choosing a familiar emotional
experience, and familiar does not always
mean healthy.
A lot of people think they're attracted
to chemistry, but sometimes what they
call chemistry is actually recognition.
Your nervous system saying, I've been
here before. I know this feeling. I know
how to survive here.
Even if that feeling is anxiety, even if
that feeling is inconsistency,
even if that feeling is having to work
for love, especially then.
Because the nervous system doesn't ask,
is this good for me?
It asks, is this familiar?
And if chaos was familiar, peace can
feel boring.
If inconsistency was familiar, stability
can feel suspicious.
If love had to be earned, healthy love
can feel empty at first. Not because it
is empty, because it's unfamiliar,
and unfamiliar feels unsafe before it
feels safe.
That is why people can understand their
pattern completely and still repeat it.
Because insight is not the same as
change.
You can know exactly why you do this.
You can name the wound, trace it back,
see the pattern clearly, and still feel
intensely drawn to the person who
activates it.
Because this doesn't live only in your
mind, it lives in your body, in your
nervous system,
in the automatic part of you that
learned what love feels like long before
you had words for it.
So, what actually changes the pattern?
Not just awareness,
practice.
Staying long enough in what feels
unfamiliar,
letting consistency feel strange without
running from it, letting calm feel
unfamiliar without calling it boredom,
letting someone be fully present without
needing them to create anxiety for you
to feel intensity.
That's the work.
And it doesn't feel exciting. It feels
uncomfortable. It feels flat, quiet,
unnatural.
Sometimes it even feels like loss,
because in a way, it is.
You are grieving the version of love
your body first learned to expect. You
are breaking loyalty to an old survival
pattern, and that is hard. But hard does
not mean wrong. It means you are finally
doing something different.
So, if you keep repeating the same
relationship pattern, stop asking, why
do I keep choosing the wrong person?
Ask this instead. What feeling keeps
feeling like home even when it hurts me?
Because that feeling, not the person, is
the pattern. And patterns can change,
but only when you stop following what
feels familiar and start building
familiarity with what is actually safe.
If this hit something real in you, stay
here.
