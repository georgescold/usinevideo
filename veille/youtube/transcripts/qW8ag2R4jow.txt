Hello. In this presentation, we're
looking at how Bulb's theory of
attachment has shaped realworld
scenarios and where it's faced
criticism. Let's start with its positive
impact. First, child care
practices. Bulb's research changed
hospital policies forever. Once upon a
time, parents were kept away from their
sick children for long periods of time.
But now, thanks to Bulby, hospitals now
allow parents to stay overnight, easing
children's anxiety and helping them to
heal with emotional security. Secondly,
adoption and foster care. Bulby showed
how vital early attachments are. And
because of his work, adoption agencies
now prioritize placing babies with
families as early as possible, and that
gives them the best chance at forming
healthy
bonds. Third, parenting advice. Modern
parenting draws heavily on Bulby's
ideas. skin-to-skin contact, responsive
feeding, playful interaction. These
activities are all about building secure
attachments from the start, helping
children grow into emotionally healthy
adults. And fourth, mental health
interventions. Bulb's idea of the
internal working model, that is that
early attachments shape future
relationships, has led to therapies
focused on healing attachment
wounds. Attachmentbased psychotherapy,
for instance, helps people rebuild trust
and form healthier
relationships. Now to the criticisms.
First, an overemphasis on the mother.
Bulby suggested one primary caregiver,
usually the mother, is key. Research
shows children form strong bonds with
multiple people, fathers, grandparents,
siblings, and this challenges the idea
that only one bond matters. And it
reminds us that shared caregiving is
powerful, too.
Second, we'll look at cultural
limitations. Bulb's ideas were rooted in
Western culture, thinking about Europe,
the United States, and so on. Whereas in
many societies, caregiving is a
community effort. Think about villages
for instance in Africa. And with a
community effort, children still grow up
secure and emotionally healthy.
Attachment therefore isn't just a
oneperson job. It's a collective
experience in many parts of the world.
And third, perhaps Bulb's approach was a
bit rigid as far as the critical period
is concerned. Bulby believed a child
must form a primary attachment within
the first two years or else risk
permanent damage. But later research
shows that it's not that simple. With
the right support, children who miss
early attachments can still develop
strong, secure relationships later on.
And so, in
conclusion, Bulb's theory has given us
powerful insights into early
relationships, reshaping child care,
adoption, parenting, and therapy. But
it's important to see attachment as
flexible, diverse, and deeply human.
Just like the children, it seeks to
understand.
