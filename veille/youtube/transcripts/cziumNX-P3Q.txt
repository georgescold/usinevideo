# The Five Factor Model of Personality (The Big Five)
# Foundations in Psychology · 223 vues · 00:01:48
# https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cziumNX-P3Q

The Big Five personality model,
originally proposed by the psychologists
Paul Costa and Robert McCrae, is a
trait-based approach to understanding
our personality. Let's dive in to the
five traits included in this model. The
first trait we'll discuss, openness to
experience, highlights how open-minded
and curious someone is. People high in
openness tend to appreciate art, are
creative, like to explore new ideas, and
are likely to be out ofthe- box
thinkers. The next trait is
conscientiousness.
This dimension reflects how organized
and detail oriented someone is.
Conscientious people tend to plan ahead,
are efficient, and pay attention to
detail. Now, let's talk about
extroversion.
Those who score high on this trait are
energized by being around other people.
Extroverts are social, outgoing, and
enjoy highly stimulating environments.
Those who score low on this trait are
described as introverted and may prefer
to spend time with a smaller group of
close friends. The fourth trait is
agreeableness. This trait captures how
kind and cooperative a person is.
Agreeable folks tend to be empathetic,
friendly, and willing to help others.
Those who score low on this trait may be
more assertive and competitive, but this
does not necessarily mean they are rude
or argumentative. The final trait,
neuroticism, refers to emotional
stability. Those high in neuroticism may
experience anxiety and mood swings. They
may frequently ruminate on negative
experiences. In summary, the big five
model is one way to understand our
personality and the personalities of
others. How do you measure on these
personality traits?
