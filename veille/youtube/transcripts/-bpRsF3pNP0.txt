# Swipe Fatigue Is Training You To Be Single | 30 Day Courtship Reset
# JUSTIN G · 15 vues · 00:14:11
# https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-bpRsF3pNP0

Welcome to Justin G Academy — where 
motivation ends, and discipline begins.
I’m Justin and I’m here to help everyone improve 
their lives. To love yourself the way you love
God, so you can spread that same love to others.
 Swipe fatigue isn’t a character flaw. It’s
actually a business model. Apps are literally 
rolling out AI assistants and weekly “Meet Cute”
matches to keep you swiping instead of deciding. 
That’s not romantic serendipity—that’s product
design. For the next 30 days, delete those 
apps and run a courtship plan that puts your
values—faith, family, fitness—back on the throne. 
Three rules. One calendar. Real dates. Let’s work.
[The problem] If you feel exhausted by modern 
dating, you’re not crazy. You’re normal.
Recent surveys show roughly eight out of 
ten app users report burnout. That’s the
majority—especially younger singles. Translation: 
the system is making you tired on purpose,
because tired people scroll more and commit 
less. You’re not broken; the loop is.
[The design trap] Here’s what swipe culture trains 
into you: First, endless optionality. You’re given
a slot machine of faces, so your brain holds 
out for a perfect unicorn instead of building
something with a good human. It’s variable-reward 
psychology, not love. Second, fragmented
attention. Ten conversations at once means none 
go deep. You chase dopamine pings instead of
character. Third, risk aversion. When options 
feel infinite, you never have to risk being
known. You can ghost, orbit, breadcrumb, and call 
it “talking.” That’s not dating; that’s avoidance.
And the platforms know it. Meta just shipped 
an AI “Dating Assistant” to help you “skip
the swipe,” plus a weekly surprise match to keep 
you coming back. That’s not solving the problem;
it’s optimizing the funnel.
 I’m not anti-technology. I’m
anti-training that makes you worse at love. Love 
is a skill. It needs attention, courage, honesty,
and standards. The apps can be a tool—but if 
the tool starts using you, you lay it down.
Scripture says to guard your heart because 
everything flows from it. Guard doesn’t mean
build a bunker; it means put good boundaries 
around what’s valuable. Courtship is simply
boundaries with purpose. It asks, 
“Are we walking toward covenant,
or are we just killing time?” When dating becomes 
a habit of consumption, you lose the muscle of
commitment. Time to train that muscle back.
[The 30-Day Courtship Reset] Here’s the plan.
It’s simple, measurable, and not negotiable. 
You’re not doing this to impress me—do
it to respect your future spouse, 
your future kids, and your own soul.
Rule #1 — Delete the apps for 30 days. Not pause. 
Delete. If your job depends on Instagram/TikTok,
fine—keep them, but no DMs for dating. You 
can re-install later with standards. For now,
cut the drip. The goal is silence: 
fewer inputs, stronger signal.
Rule #2 — Two in-person reps every week. Reps = 
environments where people share values and show
character in motion. Church young-adults, small 
groups, service projects, co-ed recreational
leagues, mixed fitness classes, friend-of-a-friend 
dinners. No “vibe hunting.” We’re practicing eye
contact, presence, and simple conversation. 
Two reps every week for four weeks = eight
reps total. Track them on paper.
Rule #3 — Standards visible, logistics
clear. You’re going to write three non-negotiables 
and two flexibles. Non-negotiables: faith
alignment, family-ready maturity, sobriety/health, 
whatever matters most to you. Flexibles: hobbies,
height, aesthetics—preferences, not pillars. 
Standards visible means you can say them out
loud without apology. Logistics clear means: 
ask with a plan, accept with a time. No
“sometime.” Because dates are on calendars.
[Masculine/feminine accountability] To men:
stop auditioning with cool detachment. Lead with 
clarity and care. Pick the day, pick the place,
own the outcome. Confidence is service: you carry 
the awkward weight so she doesn’t have to. To
women: receptivity isn’t passivity. Reward 
effort with clarity. If you’re interested,
say yes decisively. If you’re not, say no 
cleanly. Don’t collect attention you don’t
intend to honor. To both: grown adults don’t 
ghost. Disagree with dignity and move on.
[The weekly map (how to run the 30 
days)] Week 1 — Detox & Direction.
Delete apps. Put the phone 
on grayscale for seven days.
Write your three non-negotiables and two 
flexibles. Pray over them. Sleep on them.
Tell two close friends you’re on a courtship 
reset. Accountability kills loopholes.
Attend two in-person reps. Speak to three 
new people each time. Keep it simple:
“Hey, I’m Justin. What brought you here?” Yes, 
you’ll feel awkward. But that’s courage reps.
Week 2 — Initiate & Observe.
From your Week-1 reps, invite one person
to a low-pressure, daylight date with a clear time 
window: coffee, walk, bookstore, farmer’s market.
On the date, ask values questions early. “What 
does a good weekend look like to you?” “Who
are your people?” “What’s your faith story?”
Notice how they treat other people—the server,
the coach, the receptionist. 
That’s character, not vibes.
Week 3 — Repeat & Decide.
Two more reps. Another invite.
If someone from Week 2 felt aligned, 
go on date two with that person.
State your intent plainly by the end of date 
two: “I’m dating toward commitment. I won’t rush,
but I also won’t drift. I want to see if 
we’re compatible for something serious.”
Week 4 — Clarity or Closure.
Date three = DTR (Define the
Relationship) checkpoint. If both of you 
feel momentum and alignment on faith, family,
and lifestyle, keep walking. If not, honor 
the person with a clean close: “You’re great;
but we’re not a match. Thanks for the time.”
No situationships. No “let’s just see.” Because
courtship is movement or mercy, not mush.
[Why this works (science + culture
perspective] The platforms themselves 
are telling you what’s happening:
Facebook Dating is adding AI to “skip swiping” 
and a weekly “surprise match” because users are
burnt out. Tinder added Modes—Double Date, College 
Mode—to keep Gen Z engaged. The apps are changing
tactics because people are tired of the old loop. 
You can wait for the loop to fix you, or you can
step outside it for 30 days and fix your habits.
Media coverage across the summer and fall has
repeated the same theme: singles are fed 
up with endless swiping, shallow chats,
and never meeting. That’s not a moral 
failing; it’s environmental design. Your
reset changes the environment: fewer screens, 
more skin-in-the-game moments, faster clarity.
[Practical objections] “What if I meet no one?” 
Then the rep is your win. You trained courage
and presence. You also learned where not to 
spend time next week. “What if faith is my #1
and I’m not finding matches?” Then aim where faith 
lives. Church, service, small groups, conferences,
Christian young-adult events, family-friend 
dinners.  Another objection might be “What if
deleting apps feels extreme?” That might be the 
point. If a tool makes you weaker at the thing it
claims to help, extreme is wisdom.
[Red flags to respect]
Inconsistent effort early on. If they only 
text at 11:30 PM, they’re not serious.
Chronic ambiguity. If they dodge 
intentional talk by date three, that’s a no.
Values mismatch. Don’t missionary-date. 
“Equally yoked” isn’t a churchy slogan;
it’s a lifetime back-saver.
Non-negotiables breaking. Sobriety,
integrity, fidelity—if any crack 
shows now, it won’t fix itself later.
[Green flags to pursue]
Aligned purpose. They know why they’re
building a life, not just chasing aesthetic vibes.
Consistent kindness. Not performance—pattern.
Family enthusiasm. If talking family 
sounds like joy instead of jail, note it.
Repentance muscle. They can apologize, 
adjust, and grow. That’s covenant material.
Men, you’re not a vending machine of 
attention, and you’re not a stone wall
either. Lead gently. Pay for the first date 
without making it transactional. Ask good
questions. Practice brevity. Women, you don’t 
need to chase or test. Choose. If his effort is
steady and character is real, say yes with 
warmth. If it’s not, release with respect.
Be sure to run this plan in community. 
Tell a couple with a strong marriage what
you’re doing. Ask them for feedback 
after dates one and two: “Do you see
blind spots?” Wisdom borrowed is time saved.
[Metrics that matter] You’re going to measure
what actually predicts love:
Reps attended (goal: 8).
Invites made (goal: 2–4).
Dates completed (goal:
3 with one person or 1–2 with two people).
Clarity events (DTR “define the
relationship” by date three).
Character notes (one mental
paragraph after each date: “What did I see? 
What did I feel? Where did I lead or avoid?”)
If you’ve been “talking” to five people for 
months with zero intent… that’s not romantic
optionality. That’s fear. Fear of choosing, 
fear of being known, fear of building. God
didn’t give you a spirit of fear. He gave you 
power, love, and a sound mind. Act like it.
Here’s your one step: Delete the apps 
and book your first two reps tonight.
Text two friends to join you. Tape your three 
non-negotiables to your mirror. Then go meet
real humans with real standards in the real 
world. At 30 days, you’ll feel different:
calmer mind, cleaner inbox, clearer path. 
And if you meet someone worth building with?
You won’t need an algorithm to tell you.
