# The psychology of romance: why opposites do not actually attract
# PsyPost · 542 vues · 00:02:30
# https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TdYsSP8iY3o

The old saying claims that opposites
attract, but a new study of over 40,000
people in 74 countries suggests the
reality of romantic compatibility is a
bit more complicated.
Researchers at the University of Wroclaw
in Poland wanted to test whether people
really need to be similar to be happy in
a relationship and whether these rules
hold true across different cultures.
To find out, the researchers analyzed
survey data from individuals currently
in romantic relationships.
Participants rated themselves and their
partners on nine traits, including
kindness, physical attractiveness,
health, social class, education, and
political orientation.
They also completed questionnaires
measuring their relationship
satisfaction and levels of intimacy,
passion, and commitment.
The data showed that kindness and
physical attractiveness were the
strongest predictors of a high-quality
relationship.
For these socially desirable traits, a
pattern known as partner idealization
emerged. Participants reported the
highest levels of love and satisfaction
when they believed their partner was
kinder and more attractive than they
were.
The researchers noted that relationship
quality was highest when individuals
rated both themselves and their partner
very highly on these traits, but
perceiving the partner as slightly
better was linked to the best outcomes.
However, the pattern was completely
different for value-based traits like
political orientation.
For political beliefs, strict similarity
was the strongest predictor of a happy
relationship.
The further apart people perceived
themselves and their partners to be
politically, the lower their
relationship quality tended to be.
The researchers expected to find
differences between what men and women
prioritize, but the results showed
largely consistent patterns across
genders. Culture played a much larger
role. In highly modernized countries
with high individualism, traits like
kindness and attractiveness had the
strongest link to relationship
happiness.
In less modernized countries, matching
on social class and educational
background was a stronger predictor of
relationship stability.
Overall, the study suggests that the
idea of opposites attracting is mostly a
myth. Instead, a mix of shared values
and viewing your partner favorably seems
to be the recipe for romance. Learn more
about this study and other new research
at psychpost.org.
