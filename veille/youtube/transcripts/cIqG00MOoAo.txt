# Why Couples Slowly Drift Apart
# Bryan Vignery · 5 vues · 00:03:58
# https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cIqG00MOoAo

There's something I've learned after
years of counseling couples. Most
marriages don't fall apart all at once.
They drift. And usually the drift is so
subtle that couples don't even recognize
it while it's happening. You can still
love each other, still live in the same
house, still raise kids together, still
function as a team, and yet emotionally
feel miles apart. And one of the most
dangerous things about marriage drift is
this. What starts unhealthy slowly
starts to feel normal. The lack of
communication, the emotional distance,
>> [music]
>> the avoidance, the disconnection,
eventually couples stop expecting
closeness and start surviving. And
honestly, that's one of the reasons my
wife Stephanie and I felt so strongly
about writing the intentional marriage
book. Not because we have it all figured
out, not at all. Not because we
communicate perfectly, we don't. Not
because we've never struggled ourselves,
we're all on this journey together and
we're included in that. We've had to
learn how to communicate better, how to
slow down, how to reconnect, how to work
through frustration and misunderstanding
just like every other couple. This book
was not written from a place of
perfection, not at all. It was written
from years of counseling, years of
ministry, years of marriage, and a deep
belief that healthy marriages are built
intentionally. One of the biggest
mistakes couples make is believing their
problems are only about the other
person. But marriage has a way of
exposing what's already happening inside
of us. Our fears, our insecurities,
our wounds, our expectations, our
triggers. And what often happens is
couples get trapped in cycles. One
person reacts, the other person shuts
down. One pursues, the other withdraws.
And after enough repetition, the pattern
starts feeling permanent. But unhealthy
cycles are not permanent. They can be
understood, they can be interrupted, and
they can be rebuilt differently. That's
where intentionality begins. One of the
biggest lies couples believe is if we
were just healthier, we wouldn't
struggle. Healthy marriages are not
marriages without problems. Healthy
marriages are made up of two imperfect
people who are willing to grow. Two
people willing to communicate honestly,
take responsibility,
extend grace, heal, forgive, and keep
showing up with each other
intentionally. Marriage is not about
pretending everything is perfect. It's
about learning how to grow through what
gets revealed. And honestly, sometimes
the struggle itself becomes the
invitation to deeper connection. The
strongest marriages I've ever seen are
not usually the couples who had the
easiest story. They're couples who
decided, "We are not going to drift."
They learned to prioritize connection
with each other, to communicate
intentionally, to address problems
early, to stay emotionally engaged, to
pursue one another. Because connection
doesn't sustain itself automatically.
You don't drift into emotional intimacy,
you build it. One conversation at a
time, one intentional moment at a time,
one healthy decision at a time. That's
really the heart behind the intentional
marriage. This book isn't about
perfection. It's about helping couples
become more intentional emotionally,
spiritually, relationally, and
practically. Whether your marriage feels
like a strong place right now or whether
you feel like you've slowly drifted
apart, there is hope. Healthy marriages
can be rebuilt. Connection can grow
again, and just remember, better doesn't
happen by accident. If this message
resonates with you, I'd love for you to
check out The Intentional Marriage, A
Guide to Building Love That Lasts.
[music] And remember, a healthy marriage
isn't found, it's built intentionally.
