Day four, similarity versus opposites
attract. This is day four and today we
settle an old argument. You have heard
both sayings, birds of a feather flock
together and opposites attract. They
cannot both be right. So, which one does
science actually support? Let us find
out. Here is the story. In one of the
classic studies, psychologist Donn Byrne
gave students a questionnaire about
their attitudes, politics, music,
lifestyle, [music]
everything. Then he showed each student
a questionnaire supposedly filled in by
a stranger, but secretly Byrne had faked
the answers of the stranger. Some
matched the answers of the student
closely. Some did not. The result was
almost mathematical. [music]
The more the attitudes of the stranger
matched the student, the more the
student liked them before ever meeting
them. Byrne even called it the law of
attraction. Liking rises in a straight
line with similarity [music] and real
couples prove it, too. Researchers who
study actual married partners find they
tend to match on age, education,
religion, values, humor style, and even
how they like to spend a Sunday.
Psychologists call this homogamy, like
marrying like. Long-term studies show
that couples who share core values fight
less and stay together longer. But why
does similarity feel so good? Three
reasons. Number one, it validates us.
When someone agrees with you, it quietly
whispers, "You see the world correctly."
That is deeply comforting. Disagreement,
even small [music] disagreement, quietly
says, "One of us is wrong." Number two,
it is easier. Same food, same jokes,
same idea of a fun evening. Fewer
negotiations, fewer fights. Every shared
preference is one less battle. Number
three, we expect they will like us back.
We assume similar people will approve of
us and being liked is half of liking
someone. So, why does opposites attract
[music] feel true? Two reasons. First,
we notice the exceptions. A quiet man
with a talkative wife is a story we
remember and retell. The thousand
couples who are both quiet are
invisible. That is just our brain
spotlighting the unusual. Second, what
looks like opposites is often really a
division of labor. One partner loves
planning, the other loves going with the
flow. That works. But, look deeper and
you will usually find they agree on the
big things. Money, family, honesty.
Think of an arranged marriage
introduction where the families match
community, education, and background
first. Whatever else one thinks of the
system, it is accidentally built on the
law of bayan.
It starts [music] from similarity. Now,
think of a couple who fight constantly.
Dig underneath and it is rarely because
one likes tea and the other likes
coffee. It is because one values saving
and the other values [music] spending.
Small differences add spice. A partner
who introduces you to new music or new
food makes life bigger. So, the
research-backed formula is roughly this.
Same values, same direction in life,
different flavors. Now, your two-minute
reflection. Think of your closest
relationship. List three big things you
agree on, like money, family, or what
matters in life. And one fun way you
differ. Notice which list holds the
relationship together and which list
just keeps it interesting. See you then.
