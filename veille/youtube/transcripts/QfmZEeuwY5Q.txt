# Why You Push People Away (Even When You Want Them Close)
# Mind Tricks Explained · 15 vues · 00:02:26
# https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QfmZEeuwY5Q

You want friends. You want a
relationship. You might even complain
about being lonely. But the second
someone actually gets close, like really
close, you suddenly find them annoying.
Or you stop texting back. Or you start
an argument over how they breathe. You
think you're just independent or picky.
But in psychology, this is often a
subconscious auto-eject button.
You're pushing people away before they
have the chance to leave you. This
usually stems from your attachment
style.
If you grew up in an environment where
showing emotion was weak or where people
you relied on were inconsistent, your
brain developed a security script. Your
brain isn't trying to make you lonely.
It's trying to keep you safe. To your
subconscious, closeness equals danger.
When someone shows you genuine
affection, your internal bodyguard
panics. It thinks, "If we let them in,
they'll see the real us. And if they see
the real us, they'll eventually leave.
So let's just ruin it now while we're
still in control."
Here's the self-awareness trigger. You
might be testing people without
realizing it. You act distant or
difficult to see if they'll to prove
their loyalty by staying. But here's the
flaw in that logic. Healthy people have
boundaries. When you push them, they
don't see a test. They just see someone
who doesn't want them around. So they
leave.
Your brain then sighs in relief and
says, "See, I knew they'd leave. Good
thing I didn't get too close."
It's a self-fulfilling prophecy that
keeps you safe, but incredibly isolated.
Pushing people away is a lonely way to
stay safe.
The first step to stopping is realizing
that your independence is actually just
a very expensive suit of armor.
Next time you feel the urge to ghost
someone or pick a fight, ask yourself,
"Am I actually annoyed or am I just
scared?
Choosing to stay when you want to run is
the bravest thing a hedgehog can do.
