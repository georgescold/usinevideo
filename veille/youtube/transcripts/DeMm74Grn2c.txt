The scientist who divorced twice and
failed miserably in his own marriages
only to decide to uncover the dangerous
secret behind the collapse of any
relationship. American psychologist John
Gottman dedicated his life to studying
marriages, observing couples, their
problems, and how to fix them. After
years of research, he concluded that he
could predict divorce with an
astonishing 91% accuracy. Through his
studies, he identified the main reasons
that lead to the deterioration of a
relationship and ultimately divorce,
naming them the four horsemen of the
apocalypse.
The famous relationship expert Dr. John
Gottman was married three times and
divorced twice before meeting his
current wife, Dr. Julie Gottman, with
whom he founded the Gottman Institute
and conducted their renowned
relationship research. First, criticism.
This is not a passing complaint, but an
attack on your partner's character.
There is a huge difference between
saying, "You were late today and that
upset me." and saying, "You are always
careless and I can never rely on you."
The first focuses on the behavior, while
the second attacks their identity. When
this pattern repeats, the other person
feels rejected as a human being, not
just for a specific situation. Second,
contempt. This is considered the most
dangerous horseman. It shows up in
sarcasm, mocking, belittling, or even
provocative body language, like rolling
your eyes or smirking. Contempt doesn't
just communicate anger.
It sends a hidden message that says,
"I am better than you." Gottman found
that this behavior is the strongest
predictor of divorce because it destroys
the foundation of mutual respect. Third,
defensiveness. When a conversation turns
into an interrogation, one partner often
resorts to defensiveness.
Instead of taking partial
responsibility, they start shifting the
entire blame onto the other person.
"I didn't do anything wrong. It's your
fault." This stops all listening.
And the discussion turns into an
exchange of accusations. Defensiveness
prevents repair because it closes the
door to admitting mistakes. Fourth,
stonewalling. This is complete emotional
withdrawal. One partner goes silent,
walks away from the conversation, and
avoids eye contact, as if building a
wall between them. Silence might
sometimes seem like a way to protect
oneself from exploding,
but if it becomes a habit, it creates a
deep emotional gap. Don't forget to
subscribe so your relationship doesn't
collapse without you noticing, and to
always stay one step ahead of the
problems.
