# Why Relationships Keep Failing
# Still Awareness · 40 vues · 00:04:24
# https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KK7tcvEHaYI

I was watching a video on YouTube about
relationships and what really stood out
to me was the comment section where many
people seem to be struggling with the
same issues. Some dealing with a lack of
maturity from their partner while others
find it hard to meet the right person.
And interestingly, it's not because
there are no options but the opposite as
dating apps have created endless choices
that lead to a sense of overwhelm and
emotional saturation while at the same
time expectations have become extremely
high. With people no longer just looking
for a partner but for someone perfect.
Almost like a savior who will fix and
complete their lives.
Love in the neighborhood.
In the past, marrying the girl next door
felt like something very simple. A small
neighborhood, the same faces every day.
People who had known each other since
childhood. When a young man or woman
grew up, they didn't need dating apps or
hundreds of options. The choice was
already there from the beginning. Close,
clear, and familiar. But here's the
question. Does that make love less
meaningful or more stable? Back [snorts]
then, people weren't searching for the
perfect person. No one was saying, "I
want someone who has every possible
quality." The idea was simpler. This
person is suitable. We can build a life
together and that was it. You might ask,
"But where's the passion? Where's the
intense feeling at first sight?" It's
true. Love didn't always start like a
burning storm. Sometimes it began
quietly, like warm water, not boiling.
But over time, that warmth turned into
something real built on respect, shared
experiences, and patience. Today, we
don't live in small neighborhoods. We
live in an open world with no limits.
Every day, there's someone new, a new
option. And the question is no longer,
"Who is right for me?" but is there
someone better?
Sometimes having too many choices
doesn't make us happier. It makes us
more hesitant. And if you ask me whether
marrying the girl next door was better,
I'd say not necessarily better, but it
was simpler. And sometimes simplicity is
exactly what we're missing today.
Singlehood crisis.
Today, finding love, let alone marriage,
is no longer as guaranteed as it once
was.
In many Western countries, and even in
places like Tunisia, there seems to be a
kind of singlehood crisis, where many
people struggle to find a partner.
Family and relatives no longer play a
significant role in this process. And
it's no longer common to marry someone
from your immediate environment. Why
would you when you can meet people
outside your comfort zone, at work or
while traveling?
At the same time, we've become extremely
demanding, looking for a partner who is
perfect. Someone who is a friend, a
parent, and a sibling all at once, who
provides stability, yet is also fun and
adventurous.
These expectations place immense
pressure on both sides.
As the psychologist Alain de Botton puts
it, we are not just looking for love,
but for someone to heal our
psychological wounds.
So, the question is, are we searching
for a partner or a savior?
Better relationships.
I'm not an expert in relationships or
anything like that, but based on the
issues we talked about, here are a few
steps that might help.
First, reduce the constant comparisons
and endless alternatives, and focus more
on real-life connections that grow
slowly over time.
Second, instead of relying entirely on
dating apps, it's better to build
relationships in real life through the
gym, social activities, volunteering, or
any hobby because genuine connection
grows stronger with repeated interaction
in a healthy environment. Third, develop
emotional independence and avoid placing
all of your psychological, social, and
emotional needs on your partner.
