# The avoidant SLOW FADE discard
# Coach Ryan · 6,9 k vues · 00:06:06
# https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S45eJ8h2BZI

Not all breakups with avoidance are
abrupt blindsiding discards. Many of
them are the slow fade where this person
is pulling back, pulling back, pulling
back, emotionally withdrawing more and
more from the relationship. And you feel
them slipping through your fingers. And
the slow fade is often more painful and
more anxiety producing than the abrupt
discard.
And the reason why dismissive avoidance
and fearful avoidance will do the slow
fade out is because their fears have
been triggered, but they're trying to
get you to be the one to end the
relationship. Now, they'll slow fade
out, slow fade out, slow fade out, and
you'll ask them, "Is everything okay?"
They'll tell you everything's fine, but
you can feel them withdrawing physically
and emotionally from the relationship.
Intimacy becomes virtually non-existent.
And eventually it reaches the point
where they finally end it mercifully or
you cannot take it anymore and your
nervous system is fried. You're sick of
being an anxious mess and you cut it
off. Finally holding that boundary,
which is what they wanted you to do in
this instance. Because if you end the
relationship and you cut it off, you're
the bad guy. They're not the bad guy.
They get to escape accountability for
destroying the relationship because
you're the one that ended it. They get
to be the victim. Now, accountability is
so painful for avoidance because of a
core wound of shame. Shame being that
I'm defective. I'm not lovable emotion
and it's a toxic emotion. And even
taking the slightest accountability,
well, to them, it's the emotional
equivalent of confirming their own
defectiveness. It's so painful. So by
making you pull that trigger, they get
to escape that accountability.
Now rather than communicating with you
about their fears, about their feelings,
and giving the relationship an
opportunity to work out, they shut down
and withdraw because communicating risks
conflict. And communicating about this
stuff means vulnerability. And conflict
and vulnerability feel deeply unsafe to
them. And that stems from consistent
profound childhood emotional neglect.
They were not given that love and
nurturing that they needed. But in
addition to that neglect, their feelings
were sharply rejected, brutally
rejected. And the message was sent to
them, your emotions aren't welcome. And
if you dare try to express your
emotions, you're going to get rejected.
And that rejection to them is
confirmation of their own shame. So
rather than communicating their
feelings, fears and needs to you, they
get so overwhelmed with anxiety at the
thought of even communicating that or at
the thought of the growing closeness and
intimacy and vulnerability that the only
way they know to regulate their nervous
system and feel safe again is to create
emotional distance. Because if there's
emotional distance between you and them,
well, that way you can't get too close
and figure out that they're defective.
Because if they let you in and you get
close, their subconscious fear is my
partner, if I let them in my heart, my
partner is going to figure out that I'm
not lovable. They're going to figure out
I'm defective and they're going to
reject me. And in the case of fearful
avoidance, add in betrayal. They've
learned from childhood that love equals
pain. And anybody that you love and get
close to is ultimately going to betray
and hurt you. So, their fears have been
triggered. And rather than communicating
them, rather than working them out, they
withdraw. And in many cases, they'll
even do the mental gymnastics, telling
themselves, "If my partner really loved
me, if they cared about me, they would
just know what I feel. They would just
know what I want." Escaping
accountability for their lack of
communication by making it your fault
for not knowing what they never told
you. But ultimately, it's everything to
do with their attachment wounds and
their inability to communicate as well
as their emotional unavailability.
Because emotional availability requires
the ability and the willingness to
empathize with your partner, to share
their emotions, to care about their
feelings. You know, the whole thought
process of how do my actions impact this
person? But when they're slow fading
out, they're not thinking of you.
They're not thinking of the emotional
damage that they're causing to you.
They're thinking of themselves and
whatever it is that they perceive they
want, need, feel, or fear in the moment.
But you don't have to endure the slow
fade out. It's far too painful to
subject yourself to. Hold your
boundaries. Your boundaries are your
limits of what you'll allow, what you
won't allow, what you'll subject
yourself to. You can't control another
person. And boundaries are not about
controlling the other person. They're
about you controlling your side of the
street. So when they're slow fading out
and you can feel that distance and that
lack of effort and that a lack of
emotional connection, this is where you
hold the boundary and simply say, "I've
been noticing a lot of distance between
us and a lot of inconsistency.
Is this relationship something that you
are interested in continuing?" Because
this kind of distance is not something
that works for me. And in order for me
to feel safe, I need to see mutual
effort and communication and consistency
rather than the drift apart. There you
go. Now, the relationship might end, but
if it does, you ended it and it ended in
a way where you preserved your dignity
and respected your worth because you
don't need their validation to be worth
something. You don't need them to choose
you. If they can't show up for you and
they are emotionally unavailable and
can't communicate, then your your worth
is not dependent on that of a person
that is emotionally immature and stunted
that can't communicate. That's an
emotional child. They don't get to
determine your worth. But you can
respect your worth and show up for
yourself and hold boundaries because
without boundaries, the slow fade out
will just be prolonged and be that much
more painful and you'll lose yourself in
the process becoming more and more
anxious. Don't do that to yourself. Show
up for yourself. Don't subject yourself
to the slow fade ever again.
